Personalized Training
The debrief, captured inside the workflow

A pilot trains every six to nine months, usually with a different instructor. The system records that the session happened, the date and the grade. Almost none of the conversation survives, and the conversation is where the training was.

What we thinkThe debrief can be captured inside the workflow, drafted, and approved by the instructor before any of it becomes official. The next instructor walks in with the full picture, the trainee gets a refresher months later, and gaps that repeat across the programme can finally be counted.
Questions to start on
  1. How does what one instructor learns about a trainee reach the next instructor today?
  2. What happens to the conversation in your debriefs. Who, if anyone, writes it down?
  3. When an instructor meets a trainee for the first time, what would they need to know before walking in?
  4. What analysis would you run tomorrow if the data existed, that you cannot run today?
  5. What would have to be in place before you were comfortable recording a debrief?
Virtual Teammates
A teammate you hire, not a tool you configure

The platform gives your team good tools, and somebody still has to operate every one of them. Approving documents. Chasing expiries. Working out how many slots and for whom. One national training operator put the share of team time spent making processes run, rather than improving them, at about 60 percent, and said that was probably conservative.

What we thinkA teammate you hire rather than a tool you configure. It takes a job description in plain language, raises its hand when it is unsure, and leaves an audit trail behind every action. Nothing leaves the organisation without a person approving it.
Questions to start on
  1. Which recurring process cost your team the most time this month?
  2. Which of those would you hand over tomorrow?
  3. Which would you never hand over?
  4. What would you need to see before you trusted it with a process?
  5. If your team got one day a week back, what would you do with it?
Insights and Workflows
Ask in plain language. Build without a developer

Asking your data a question needs report-builder skill or SQL. Building a process needs a developer. It is the same bottleneck twice, and usually the same one person. Most organisations also cannot say what their existing automations actually do: the handler works, nobody has touched it in four years, and the person who wrote it has left.

What we thinkAsk in plain language and get the answer, on the platform you run today. Describe an automation in plain language and get a draft that a person reviews and activates. And read an existing handler back in business language, read-only, so it documents what is configured without changing it.
Questions to start on
  1. What question do you ask most often that the system cannot answer without help?
  2. What is sitting in your reporting backlog right now, and how long has it been there?
  3. Which process does your team still run by hand that everyone agrees should be automated?
  4. What happens when the person who builds your automations goes on holiday?
  5. If everyone could ask the system anything, any time, would you still build and maintain reports?
The Modernization Journey
No demo. What would make it work where you are

You run Fox, MINT or Comply365 Training today. This is how we have said the journey works.

  • We come to you. In 2027, 2028 or 2029, when the modernized capabilities already cover what you use today.
  • The plan is agreed together. What data migrates and when, and how your teams work through it. Nothing starts until both sides sign.
  • You set the pace. Fleet by fleet, department by department. No big bang, and no switch-off date you have not agreed.
  • Nobody moves on our schedule. Which wave you are in is a conversation, not an allocation.
What we are askingWhat would have to be true for this to work where you are. What is missing from the plan. What you would need from us, and when, for this to be something your organisation could commit to.
Questions to start on
  1. What would have to be true in your operation before you would want to start?
  2. What is missing from this plan that you would need before you could commit to it?
  3. What would make the move succeed where you work, and what would make it fail?
  4. What do you need from us, and how far ahead, to plan around this?
  5. Who decides this where you work, and what do they need in order to say yes?
The useful half of this hour is what would make these work in a real operation. We know what we like about them. What we cannot get anywhere else is what you would need, and what gets in the way. Nothing said here is attributed to a person or an airline.

What to bring back

  1. What rings true in your operation.
  2. What you would need that is not there.
  3. The one thing you would change first.
